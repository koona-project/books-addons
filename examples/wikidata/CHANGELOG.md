# Changelog

## 1.0.1 — 2026-11-24

- Finds books only. An item counts when Wikidata says it is a work of writing or an edition (P31: literary work, written work, version/edition/translation, book, novel, light novel, textbook and the other classes listed in `info.lua`), when it is an edition of (P629) such a work, or when it has no class at all but has an ISBN. Films, TV films and series, operas, characters and people of the same name are left out, and so are their posters among the covers.
- Each search asks for 20 hits instead of 10, since the items that are not books take many of them. The searches (full text with the first author, then with the second, then by name in the book's language and in English) are tried in turn until one finds a book by the author asked for, or Wikidata asks to slow down. A batch of items too large to answer is asked again in halves, and a batch that fails leaves out only its own items. A lookup makes 60 requests at most. When nothing is found and a request failed, the answer is the failure, not "nothing found". The title and author go to the full-text search as plain words.
- When the book has an author, a candidate whose authors (its own P50, or its work's) are known and are others is left out instead of being shown as "only the title matches". Names are compared in every language fetched, labels and aliases, in any word order, decomposed or composed; a spelling in another script is compared by its consonants in Latin letters, an initial standing for a word that starts with it; a Chinese, Japanese or Korean name is the same with or without spaces, and with its two words in either order; a short word ("Li", "Lu") is compared whole. A surname alone among Wikidata's aliases is not taken for another person of that surname; a one-word label ("Colette") is a whole name when the library's name has it as the surname. Greek is compared in any case and with or without accents; 々 is a letter of a Japanese name. A candidate with no known author is kept only when no candidate is by the author asked for.
- The names of authors, publishers, series and genres are fetched with their aliases: the authors' first, to see whose books were found, the rest only for the rows kept. A batch of names that fails keeps the names fetched before it. An author whose name on Wikidata is exactly the library's is shown under that name, with the label among the other names (ten at most).
